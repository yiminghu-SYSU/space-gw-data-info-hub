## 信息摘要

MicroBH 项目旨在连接弦理论与引力波、黑洞观测：构造首批达到视界尺度的天体物理黑洞微观态几何，使用数值相对论模拟其非线性演化，并计算多极矩、潮汐效应、准正规模、回声、并合波形及黑洞成像等可观测特征，与 LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA、Einstein Telescope、Cosmic Explorer、LISA 和 Event Horizon Telescope 的当前及未来观测进行比较。四个节点将组成紧密协作的国际团队，通过互访、年度线下会议和定期远程会议共同开展研究。

## 完整通知原文

Dear Colleagues,

We expect to open several postdoctoral positions within the new international project “The Microscopic Structure of Black Holes” (MicroBH), recently funded by the John Templeton Foundation.

MicroBH brings together four groups with complementary expertise, with the aim of building a bridge from string theory to gravitational-wave (GW) and black-hole observations:

- IPhT, CEA Paris-Saclay, France  
  Prof. Iosif Bena  
  <https://www.ipht.fr/en/string-theory/>
- Sapienza University of Rome, Italy  
  Prof. Paolo Pani  
  <https://gmunu.web.uniroma1.it/>
- DAMTP, University of Cambridge, UK  
  Prof. Jorge Santos  
  <https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/research/hep/person/jss55>
- University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, USA (lead institution)  
  Prof. Helvi Witek  
  <https://witek-gravity-group.physics.illinois.edu/>

The project aims to construct the first horizon-scale microstate geometries of astrophysical black holes, to evolve their nonlinear dynamics with numerical-relativity simulations, and to compute their observable signatures (multipole moments, tidal effects, quasinormal modes, echoes, merger waveforms and black-hole images), to be confronted with current and future GW detectors (LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA, Einstein Telescope, Cosmic Explorer, LISA) and with the Event Horizon Telescope.

Two postdoctoral researchers are expected to be hired at each of the four sites through a coordinated selection process, subject to each institution’s recruitment procedures. Candidates with expertise in string theory and supergravity, black-hole physics, numerical relativity, numerical methods for PDEs, or gravitational-wave modelling and phenomenology are particularly encouraged to apply. Applicants may indicate their preferred host institution(s).

Appointments are for two years, with a possible extension subject to satisfactory performance and availability of funds, and a salary competitive with international standards. Candidates must hold, or be about to complete, a Ph.D. in Physics or a related field, and must have completed their PhD before the start of the position. The positions are expected to start in August/September 2027, although earlier start dates can be considered.

The deadline for expressions of interest through AJO is 15 December 2026. Screening will begin in December 2026 and will continue until the positions are filled.
